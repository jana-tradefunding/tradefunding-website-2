What Is a Business Term Loan?

A business term loan provides a lump sum upfront, repaid through fixed instalments over a set period — typically 3 months to 7 years, depending on the lender and loan size. Repayments follow an amortisation schedule: each instalment covers a portion of principal and interest, calculated so the loan is fully repaid by the end of the term.

Unlike a line of credit or overdraft, a term loan is a one-off draw rather than a revolving facility. That structure suits a single, defined use — buying equipment, funding an expansion, or consolidating existing debt — rather than ongoing working capital needs.

Understanding the True Cost: APR vs. Headline Rate

A lower headline rate doesn't always mean a cheaper loan. Fees change the real cost more than most borrowers expect — especially on shorter terms, where a one-off origination fee is spread across fewer repayments. Worked example: $150,000 loan, 12-month term.

LENDER A
7.9% p.a.
Origination fee: 4% ($6,000)
Facility fee: $50/month ($600/yr)
Total cost: $18,450
Effective rate: ≈12.3%
LENDER B
9.2% p.a.
Origination fee: 1% ($1,500)
Facility fee: None
Total cost: $15,300
Effective rate: ≈10.2%

Lender B carries the higher headline rate but costs $3,150 less over the year.

Real-World Case Study

MELBOURNE HOSPITALITY

A Melbourne café owner refinancing a $180,000 fit-out loan compared their bank's rollover offer against the open market before committing. CBA's renewal came in at 14.5% p.a.; Judo Bank, a second-tier lender, offered 10.2% p.a. on the same security. Switching lenders saved $10,800 in interest over the remaining term — the saving alone outweighed the cost and admin of refinancing.
