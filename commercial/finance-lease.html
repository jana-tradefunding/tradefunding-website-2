FAQ

Frequently asked questions

The key difference is ownership. With a finance lease, you have the option (and usually the intention) to own the asset at the end of the term by paying a nominal residual. The asset appears on your balance sheet as both an asset and a liability. With an operating lease, you simply use the asset and return it at the end of the term. There is no ownership transfer. Operating leases typically have lower monthly payments but you don’t build equity in the asset. Finance leases suit businesses that want to own their equipment long-term, while operating leases suit those who want flexibility to upgrade.
With a finance lease, the lessee (you) can generally claim tax deductions for depreciation of the asset and the interest component of the lease payments. However, unlike a chattel mortgage, you cannot claim the GST credit upfront. GST is included in each lease payment. The depreciation method and rate depend on the asset type and its effective life as determined by the ATO. Always consult your accountant for advice specific to your situation.
A balloon payment (also called a residual value) is a lump sum due at the end of the finance lease term. By setting a balloon, you reduce your regular monthly payments during the lease. At the end of the term, you pay the balloon amount to take full ownership of the asset, refinance the balloon, or in some cases return the asset. Balloon percentages typically range from 10% to 50% of the asset value.
Finance leases can be used for many identifiable business assets with a useful economic life. Common assets include vehicles (cars, utes, trucks), construction equipment (excavators, loaders, cranes), manufacturing machinery, medical and dental equipment, hospitality fitouts, agricultural machinery, IT infrastructure, and office equipment. The key requirement is that the asset must be identifiable, have a useful life that extends beyond the lease term, and retain some residual value.
At the end of a finance lease, you typically have three options. First, you can pay the residual amount and take full ownership of the asset. This is the most common choice. Second, you can refinance the residual over an additional period if you need more time to pay. Third, you can return the asset to the lender, though this is less common with finance leases as the intention is usually ownership. Some lenders may also allow you to upgrade to a new asset and roll the residual into a new lease.
