Frequently asked questions.

What is a factor rate and how does it differ from an interest rate?

A factor rate is a decimal number, such as 1.25, that you multiply by your advance amount to get your total repayment. Unlike an interest rate, a factor rate doesn't account for time — whether you repay in 6 months or 12 months, you pay the same total amount, so repaying faster increases your effective annual cost.

How much does a merchant cash advance really cost?

The true cost depends on the factor rate and how quickly you repay. Factor rates typically range from 1.1 to 1.5. Because MCAs are often repaid within 6–12 months, the effective annualised cost can run well above what a term loan or line of credit would charge. Your Compare Report shows the real factor rate for each matched lender.

What is the daily holdback and how does it work?

The daily holdback is the percentage of your daily card sales, typically 10% to 25%, that's automatically deducted by your payment processor to repay the advance. There's no fixed monthly instalment: on a slow trading day you repay less, and on a strong day you repay more.

Can I get a merchant cash advance with bad credit?

Often, yes. Because repayment is tied to your card sales rather than a fixed monthly commitment, many MCA providers place more weight on your trading history and turnover than on your credit file.

Is a merchant cash advance technically a loan?

Technically, no. An MCA is structured as a purchase of future receivables rather than a loan, which is part of why it's priced with a factor rate instead of an interest rate.
