Frequently asked questions.

What is export finance and how does it work?

Export finance covers financial products designed to fund your international sales, from pre-shipment production costs to post-shipment receivables. A lender provides working capital so you can manufacture, process, or procure goods for export before receiving payment from your overseas buyer. Facilities are typically repaid when the buyer settles, usually within 30 to 180 days of shipment.

What is the difference between pre-shipment and post-shipment export finance?

Pre-shipment finance funds the production or procurement costs before goods are shipped, covering raw materials, manufacturing, packaging, and logistics. Post-shipment finance bridges the gap between shipping goods and receiving payment from the overseas buyer. Many exporters use both to cover the entire export cycle.

What are letters of credit and how do they relate to export finance?

A letter of credit (LC) is a guarantee from your buyer's bank that you will be paid once specific shipping conditions are met. LCs reduce the risk of non-payment from overseas buyers and can make it easier to obtain export finance. Many lenders will advance funds against a confirmed LC at lower rates than unsecured export finance.

What is Export Finance Australia and how can they help?

Export Finance Australia (formerly EFIC) is the Australian Government's export credit agency. They provide finance, guarantees, and insurance to support Australian exporters when the private market cannot fully meet their needs, including direct loans, guarantees to banks, and buyer credit facilities. They are particularly useful for SME exporters and transactions in emerging markets.
