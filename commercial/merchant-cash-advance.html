Important: MCAs Can Be Very Expensive

Merchant cash advances are often the most expensive form of business finance available. Effective annual rates can range from 30% to 150%+.

MCA lenders typically quote a "factor rate" (e.g., 1.3) rather than an interest rate, which makes it very difficult to compare to other finance products. This is exactly why Trade Funding's free Compare Report is so valuable. We translate factor rates into effective APRs so you can see the real cost and compare to cheaper alternatives.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

A factor rate is a decimal number (e.g., 1.3) that you multiply by your advance amount to get your total repayment. For example, $50,000 x 1.3 = $65,000 total repayment. Unlike an interest rate, a factor rate doesn’t account for time. Whether you repay in 6 months or 12 months, you pay the same total. This makes factor rates misleading because the faster you repay, the higher your effective annual rate.
The true cost depends on the factor rate and how quickly you repay. Factor rates typically range from 1.1 to 1.5. Because MCAs are repaid quickly (often 6–12 months), the effective APR can range from 30% to 150%+. This makes MCAs among the most expensive forms of business finance available. Our Compare Report translates factor rates into effective APRs so you can compare properly.
The daily holdback is the percentage of your daily revenue that’s automatically deducted to repay the advance. Typical holdback rates range from 10% to 25%. On busy days you repay more, on quiet days less. While this flexibility sounds appealing, the daily deductions can significantly impact your operating cash flow.
Yes, this is one of the main reasons businesses choose MCAs. Because repayment is tied to your revenue rather than fixed monthly payments, many MCA providers are more lenient on credit history. However, poorer credit typically means higher factor rates. Before accepting an expensive MCA, it’s worth checking if you qualify for cheaper alternatives through a Compare Report.
Depending on your situation, alternatives include: unsecured business term loans (typically 6%–25% p.a.), business lines of credit (8%–20% p.a.), invoice finance (if you have B2B invoices), or even equipment finance for specific purchases. Many businesses don’t realise they qualify for these cheaper products. Our Compare Report always includes alternatives alongside MCA options.
Technically, no. An MCA is structured as a purchase of future receivables, not a loan. This distinction has regulatory implications: MCAs may not be subject to the same consumer and business lending regulations as traditional loans. This is one reason why MCA providers can charge such high effective rates.
Yes, this is one of the biggest risks. Because daily holdbacks reduce your operating cash flow, some businesses find they need to take out a second MCA to cover operating expenses, creating a cycle of increasingly expensive borrowing. If you’re considering a second or third MCA, we strongly recommend getting a Compare Report to explore consolidation options.
MCAs are among the fastest forms of business finance. Many providers can approve and fund within 24–48 hours, with some same-day options available. However, if your need isn’t truly urgent, taking an extra few days to explore cheaper options through a Compare Report could save you thousands.
MCAs are most popular in industries with high daily card transaction volumes: hospitality, retail, salons and beauty services, gyms and fitness centres, and medical/dental practices. However, “popular” doesn’t mean “best”. Many of these businesses qualify for significantly cheaper finance options.
