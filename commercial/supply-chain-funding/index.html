Frequently asked questions.

What is supply chain finance and how does it work?

Supply chain finance is a set of financing solutions that optimise cash flow across your supply chain. In a typical arrangement, you (the buyer) approve supplier invoices on a technology platform, and a funder pays the supplier early, often at a small discount. You then repay the funder on extended payment terms. Suppliers get paid faster and you extend your own payment terms and preserve working capital. It's sometimes called reverse factoring, because it's initiated by the buyer rather than the supplier.

What is the difference between reverse factoring and supply chain finance?

Reverse factoring is a specific type of supply chain finance where the buyer arranges the facility and invites suppliers to participate, with suppliers choosing whether to take early payment on approved invoices at a discount. Supply chain finance is the broader term, and can also include dynamic discounting, inventory finance, and pre-shipment finance. In practice, the two terms are often used interchangeably.

How do suppliers benefit from supply chain finance?

Suppliers are typically paid within 5 days of invoice approval, instead of waiting the usual 30, 60 or 90 days. Because pricing is based on the buyer's credit rating rather than the supplier's, suppliers access funding more cheaply than they could arrange independently, and improve their cash flow predictability and days sales outstanding (DSO) along the way.

What does supply chain funding cost?

Program fees typically range from 1% to 3% of invoice value, depending on your credit profile, the length of the payment extension, and the volume of invoices run through the program. There may also be platform or facility establishment fees, which are often offset by the payment term extensions and early payment discounts you capture as the buyer.
