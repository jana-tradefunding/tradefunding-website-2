Business Charge Cards in Australia: The Complete Guide

Published 5 April 202614 min readBy Trade Funding

Business charge cards occupy a unique space in Australian business finance. Unlike a credit card that lets you carry a balance and pay interest, a charge card must be paid in full each month. In return, it gives you 30 to 55 days of interest-free float on every purchase, often with no preset spending limit and significantly better rewards than any credit card on the market.

For businesses that spend heavily on travel, advertising, supplier payments, or subscriptions, a charge card can effectively provide short-term working capital at zero cost while earning points, cash back, or travel benefits worth thousands of dollars per year. This guide covers everything Australian business owners need to know about charge cards in 2026: how they work, who offers them, what they cost, and whether one is right for your business.

What Is a Business Charge Card?

A business charge card is a payment card issued to a business that requires the full balance to be paid each statement period. There is no minimum payment option. There is no revolving balance. There is no interest charge, because carrying a balance is not permitted.

This fundamental difference from a credit card changes the economics entirely. Credit card issuers make most of their money from interest on unpaid balances. Charge card issuers make their money from annual fees and merchant transaction fees. Because charge card holders are required to pay in full, they represent lower risk to the issuer, which is why charge cards often come with better rewards, higher spending power, and premium benefits.

How a Business Charge Card Works

  1. You make purchases: Use the card for business expenses: supplies, travel, advertising, subscriptions, supplier payments.
  2. Statement closes: At the end of your billing cycle (typically monthly), you receive a statement showing all charges.
  3. Payment due: You have a set number of days (usually 14 to 21 days after the statement date) to pay the full balance. This gives you 30 to 55 days of interest-free credit from the purchase date.
  4. Balance clears: Once paid, your spending capacity resets.
The Cash Flow Advantage

The 30 to 55 day interest-free period is the core working capital benefit. If you purchase $50,000 of stock on day 1 of your billing cycle, you have up to 55 days before payment is due. If your customers pay within 30 days, you have already collected the revenue before the charge card bill arrives. You have effectively used the card issuer's money for free.

Charge Card vs Credit Card: What Is the Difference?

Many business owners confuse charge cards with credit cards. Here is a clear breakdown:

Amex Acceptance in Australia

The traditional concern with American Express is merchant acceptance. In 2026, Amex acceptance in Australia has improved significantly. Most major retailers, airlines, hotels, and online merchants accept it. However, some smaller suppliers, tradespeople, and B2B vendors still do not. If your primary spend is with suppliers who do not accept Amex, a fintech card on the Visa or Mastercard network may be more practical.

Charge Card Fees and Costs

Because charge cards do not charge interest (there is no revolving balance), the fee structure is different from credit cards:

  • Annual card fee: The primary cost. Ranges from $0 (fintech cards) to $1,750 (Amex Business Platinum). Additional employee cards typically cost $0 to $50 each per year.
  • Late payment fee: If you fail to pay your balance in full by the due date, expect a fee of $40 to $50 plus potential restrictions on your account. Amex may also charge a late payment charge calculated as a percentage of the overdue amount.
  • Foreign transaction fee: Most traditional Amex cards charge 3% on international purchases. Some Amex cards and most fintech cards offer 0% foreign transaction fees, a major benefit for businesses that buy from overseas suppliers.
  • Cash advance fee: Some cards allow cash advances, typically at a fee of 3% to 5% of the amount. This is not a feature of most charge cards and should be avoided.
  • Additional card fee: Employee cards are often free or $50 to $100 per additional card per year.

Is the Annual Fee Worth It?

This depends on your spending volume and how well you utilise the benefits. Here is a quick calculation for the Amex Business Explorer ($149 annual fee, 2 points per $1):

  • At $5,000 per month business spend: 120,000 points per year, worth approximately $480 in travel redemptions. Net gain after fee: ~$331.
  • At $15,000 per month: 360,000 points per year, worth approximately $1,440. Net gain: ~$1,291.
  • At $30,000 per month: 720,000 points per year, worth approximately $2,880. Net gain: ~$2,731.

The higher your monthly spend, the more value you extract. If your business spends less than $3,000 per month, a no-fee fintech card may be a better choice.

Pros and Cons of Business Charge Cards

Advantages

  • 30 to 55 days interest-free float on every purchase
  • No preset spending limit (Amex), scales with your business
  • Superior rewards compared to credit cards
  • No risk of accumulating revolving debt
  • Enforces spending discipline (must pay in full)
  • Premium benefits: lounge access, travel insurance, concierge
  • Built-in expense management and reporting tools
  • Employee cards with individual spending controls

Disadvantages

  • Must pay balance in full each month, with no flexibility to spread payments
  • Higher annual fees than most credit cards
  • Late payment consequences are severe (fees, account restrictions)
  • Amex not accepted by all merchants, especially smaller suppliers
  • Not suitable for businesses with irregular cash flow
  • Does not replace a loan or line of credit for larger funding needs
  • Cash advance facilities are expensive if available

Best Use Cases for a Business Charge Card

1. Business Travel

A consulting firm in Sydney sends partners to client sites across Australia and Southeast Asia. Monthly travel spend averages $18,000 (flights, hotels, meals). Using the Amex Business Platinum, they earn 40,500 points per month plus access to airport lounges, travel insurance, and a $400 annual travel credit. Annual rewards value: approximately $16,200 in travel redemptions against a $1,750 fee.

2. Advertising and Marketing Spend

A direct-to-consumer ecommerce brand spends $25,000 per month on Google Ads, Meta Ads, and influencer partnerships. Using the Amex Business Explorer ($149 fee), they earn 600,000 points annually, worth approximately $2,400 in travel. The 45-day float means they collect revenue from the campaigns before the card bill is due.

3. Supplier Payments and Inventory

A boutique wine retailer in Adelaide purchases $40,000 of stock monthly from domestic distributors who accept Amex. The charge card provides 30 to 55 days of free credit, effectively giving the retailer interest-free inventory financing while earning substantial points.

4. Employee Expense Management

A growing tech startup with 30 employees issues Weel virtual cards with per-card budgets for software subscriptions, client entertainment, and office supplies. Each card has a preset limit ($500 per month for subscriptions, $200 for entertainment), automated receipt capture, and direct integration with Xero. The finance team saves approximately 8 hours per month in expense reconciliation.

Tips for Maximising Your Charge Card Value

  1. Route all eligible spending through the card. The more you spend on the card, the more rewards you earn and the longer your cash stays in your business account earning interest.
  2. Pay on time, every time. Late payment fees are expensive, and repeated late payments can result in account cancellation. Set up a direct debit for the full balance.
  3. Use employee cards strategically. Issue cards to key team members with appropriate limits. This centralises spending, simplifies expense reporting, and earns rewards on spend that would otherwise go through personal cards or petty cash.
  4. Choose the right card for your spend profile. If you travel frequently, Amex's premium cards justify their fees. If your spend is primarily domestic and operational, a no-fee fintech card may deliver better net value.
  5. Monitor Amex acceptance. Before committing to Amex, check that your main suppliers accept it. If 40% or more of your spend goes to merchants that do not accept Amex, you will not maximise the card's value.
  6. Redeem points strategically. Points are generally worth more when redeemed for travel (flights, hotels) than for merchandise or gift cards. Transfer points to airline partners for the best value per point.
