The best business line of credit options in Australia, compared.

Published 27 August 2026 · 7 min read · By Trade Funding, ACL 387856
KEY TAKEAWAYS
There's no single "best" line of credit — the right one depends on limit size, how fast you need to draw, and what security you can offer.
Bank line of credit facilities win on rate, but lose on speed and flexibility — expect weeks, not days, to get one live.
Fintech lines of credit win on approval speed, often 24–48 hours from application to drawdown — but you pay more for that speed.
Matching lender tier to your actual use case beats chasing the lowest headline rate on a facility you can't get in time to use it.

How We Rank "Best"

"Best" is doing a lot of work in a headline like this, so here's exactly what we're ranking on. A line of credit is a revolving facility — you're approved for a limit, draw against it as needed, and pay interest only on the drawn balance. That structure means the right choice isn't just "lowest rate." A facility with a brilliant rate that takes six weeks to set up is useless if you need funds inside a fortnight.

We rank across five criteria, in this order of practical importance for most Trade Funding applicants: limit range (can you actually get the size you need), approval speed (how fast from application to funds available), security required (what you have to put up to qualify), drawdown flexibility (how easily you can pull funds once approved), and ongoing fees (line fees, redraw fees, and unused-limit charges that erode the headline rate).

Best For: Matching the Tier to the Job

The "best" line of credit changes depending on what you're actually optimising for. Four common scenarios, and which tier wins each one:

Best For: Fastest Approval
Fintech lenders win outright — 24–48 hours from application to a drawable limit, entirely online, no property valuation to wait on.
Best For: Lowest Rate
Big 4 Banks win on headline rate, especially with property or a general security agreement backing the facility — but budget 2–4 weeks to get there.
Best For: No Security Required
Fintech lenders again — smaller limits approved against cash flow and a director guarantee alone, no GSA or property caveat.
Best For: Large Facility Size
Big 4 Banks and Private Lenders both scale past $2M, but Private Lenders move faster for a large limit when property security is already in place.

Real-World Case Study

GOLD COAST BUILDING SUPPLIES

A Gold Coast building and trades supplies wholesaler needed to fund a seasonal stock buy-in ahead of the summer renovation rush — roughly $80,000 of additional inventory, needed on the floor within a week of the supplier's early-order discount closing. Their existing bank relationship offered a business line of credit at 10.8% p.a., but the formal application, security documentation, and valuation process meant a realistic 3-week timeline before funds were drawable — well past the discount window. They instead opened a $80,000 fintech line of credit at 19.5% p.a., approved and drawable in 36 hours against a director guarantee alone. The facility cost roughly $1,560 more in interest over the 8-week stock cycle than the bank option would have — but it captured a $4,200 early-order discount the bank timeline would have missed entirely, a net gain of around $2,640. They've since applied for a second-tier bank facility to run alongside it for the next cycle, once the seasonal timing pressure isn't a factor.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a fintech line of credit worse than a bank one?

Not worse — different. A fintech facility costs more in interest and fees, but it's live in days rather than weeks and doesn't require property security. For a time-sensitive need it can be the objectively better choice even at a higher rate; for a facility you'll hold for years with no urgency, a bank line of credit is usually cheaper over time.

Can I have more than one line of credit at once?

Yes. Many businesses run a fintech facility for fast, flexible access alongside a lower-rate bank facility for larger, planned drawdowns. Lenders will factor any existing facility's limit into serviceability when assessing a second one, whether it's drawn or not.

What limit can I actually get?

Most panels size an unsecured or GSA-only line of credit against monthly revenue and cash flow trend, commonly landing between 10–25% of annual turnover. Property security lifts the ceiling substantially, into the high six or seven figures depending on available equity.

Do I need to redraw the full limit to pay interest?

No. Interest is charged only on the drawn balance, not the approved limit. Some lenders also charge a smaller ongoing line fee or undrawn-balance fee on the unused portion — check this in the fee schedule, since it changes the real cost of holding a limit you don't fully use.

How fast can a line of credit actually be approved?

Fintech lenders commonly approve and fund within 24–48 hours based on bank-feed data alone. Specialist non-bank lenders typically take 3–7 business days. Bank and property-secured facilities usually take 1–4 weeks due to formal credit assessment and, where relevant, a property valuation.

What happens if I don't use my limit?

You generally still owe any ongoing line fee or undrawn-balance charge, but no interest accrues on funds you haven't drawn. An unused limit isn't free to hold, but it's far cheaper than drawn debt — which is why sizing the limit to your actual pattern of use matters more than maximising it.

Can I upgrade from a fintech LOC to a bank LOC later?

Yes, and it's a common sequence — use a fintech facility to solve an immediate timing problem, then apply for a lower-rate bank facility once there's no urgency and you have 6–12 months of trading history through the fintech facility to show consistent, controlled usage.

Does comparing line of credit lenders affect my credit score?

Comparing indicative rates through a broker or comparison service typically uses a soft check, which doesn't affect your credit file. Only a formal application triggers a hard inquiry — comparing the panel before applying protects your file from multiple hard inquiries.
