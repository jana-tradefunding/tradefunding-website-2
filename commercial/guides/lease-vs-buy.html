Equipment Finance: Should You Lease or Buy?

Published 23 March 2026 10 min read

Whether you need a new CNC machine, a fleet of delivery vehicles, medical imaging equipment, or IT infrastructure, the question is always the same: should you lease it or buy it outright? The answer has significant implications for your cash flow, tax position, and balance sheet, and the right choice depends on your specific business circumstances.

In Australia, equipment finance is a $100+ billion market, and there are more options available to SMEs than ever before. This guide walks through the key lease and purchase structures, compares the financial outcomes, and helps you decide which path makes the most sense for your business in 2026.

Understanding Your Options

When financing business equipment in Australia, you generally have four main structures to choose from:

1. Finance Lease

A finance lease is a long-term arrangement where the financier purchases the equipment and leases it to you for an agreed term (typically 2–5 years). You make regular lease payments and at the end of the term, you have the option to purchase the asset for a pre-agreed residual value, extend the lease, or return the equipment.

Key characteristics:

  • You do not own the asset during the lease term; the financier does
  • Payments are typically fixed, making budgeting straightforward
  • The residual value (also called a balloon) reduces your regular payments
  • GST is included in each lease payment rather than claimed upfront
  • The asset may or may not appear on your balance sheet depending on accounting standards

2. Operating Lease (Rental)

An operating lease is essentially a rental agreement. The lessor retains ownership of the equipment and bears the risk of the asset's residual value. At the end of the term, you return the equipment. There is typically no purchase option, or the purchase price is set at fair market value.

Key characteristics:

  • Lower monthly payments than a finance lease (because the lessor keeps the residual risk)
  • Entire lease payment is tax-deductible as an operating expense
  • Asset stays off your balance sheet (important for businesses managing debt ratios)
  • Best for equipment that depreciates quickly or becomes obsolete (technology, vehicles)
  • You never build equity in the asset

3. Chattel Mortgage (Equipment Loan)

A chattel mortgage is essentially a business loan secured against the equipment you are purchasing. You own the asset from day one, but the lender holds a mortgage over it until the loan is fully repaid. This is the most common way Australian businesses purchase equipment with finance.

Key characteristics:

  • You own the asset immediately and it appears on your balance sheet
  • You can claim the GST on the purchase price upfront (significant cash flow advantage)
  • Interest payments and depreciation are tax-deductible
  • Eligible assets under $20,000 may qualify for the instant asset write-off
  • You can set a residual (balloon) to reduce monthly repayments

4. Cash Purchase

Buying equipment outright with cash is the simplest option but ties up working capital that could be used elsewhere in your business. While there are no interest costs, you lose the opportunity to earn returns on that capital and you take on the full depreciation risk.

Tax Implications: What You Can Claim

The tax treatment is one of the biggest factors in the lease vs buy decision. Here is how each option works under current Australian tax law:

Finance Lease Tax Treatment

  • Depreciation is claimable on the asset (even though you do not technically own it)
  • The interest component of lease payments is tax-deductible
  • GST is claimed progressively on each lease payment
  • If you exercise the purchase option, the residual amount is added to the asset's cost base for depreciation purposes

Operating Lease Tax Treatment

  • The entire lease payment is tax-deductible as a business operating expense
  • No depreciation claim (you do not own the asset)
  • GST is claimed on each lease payment
  • Simplest tax treatment of all the options

Chattel Mortgage / Purchase Tax Treatment

  • Full GST credit claimed upfront on the purchase price (a significant cash flow benefit)
  • Depreciation is claimable over the asset's effective life
  • Interest on the loan is tax-deductible
  • Eligible assets under $20,000 may qualify for the instant asset write-off, allowing you to deduct the full purchase price in the year of acquisition

Always consult your accountant before making equipment finance decisions. The optimal structure depends on your individual tax position, GST registration status, and business circumstances.

Cash Flow Comparison: A Real-World Scenario

Cash flow impact is often more important than total cost for growing businesses. Consider a landscaping company that needs a $80,000 excavator. Here is how the first 12 months might look:

Cash purchase: $80,000 out the door on day one. Strong depreciation claim at tax time, but the business has $80,000 less working capital for wages, fuel, and materials.

Chattel mortgage (5 years, 8% p.a.): Monthly payments of approximately $1,620. The business keeps its cash reserves intact and claims the full GST credit ($7,273) upfront, which effectively reduces the cost in month one.

Finance lease (5 years, 8% p.a., 20% residual): Monthly payments of approximately $1,400. Lower than the chattel mortgage because of the residual value, but you will need to pay $16,000 or refinance at the end of the term.

Operating lease (3 years): Monthly payments of approximately $2,400 (higher per month because the term is shorter). At the end, you return the excavator and can upgrade to a newer model. No residual to worry about.

When Leasing Makes More Sense

  • Technology equipment: Computers, servers, and software become obsolete within 3–5 years. Leasing lets you upgrade regularly without being stuck with outdated equipment.
  • Vehicles: If you run a fleet, operating leases keep vehicles off your balance sheet and allow regular turnover.
  • Cash flow preservation: If you need to preserve working capital for growth, leasing avoids large upfront payments.
  • Balance sheet management: Operating leases can help keep your debt-to-equity ratio favourable, which matters if you are applying for other finance.
  • Uncertain business outlook: If you are not sure you will need the equipment long-term, a lease provides an exit path.

When Buying Makes More Sense

  • Long-life assets: Heavy machinery, commercial ovens, or specialised equipment that retains value and has a long useful life.
  • Instant asset write-off: If the asset qualifies and your tax position benefits from an immediate deduction, buying can deliver significant tax savings.
  • Customised equipment: If the asset is purpose-built for your business and has limited resale value, ownership makes more sense than leasing.
  • Strong cash reserves: If you have surplus cash and limited alternative investment opportunities, buying avoids interest costs entirely.
  • GST-registered businesses: Claiming the full GST credit upfront via a chattel mortgage provides an immediate cash flow boost.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it better to lease or buy business equipment in Australia?

It depends on your situation. Leasing is generally better if you want to preserve cash flow, need equipment that becomes obsolete quickly, or want to keep assets off your balance sheet. Buying is better if you plan to use the equipment for many years, want to build equity, or want to claim the instant asset write-off tax benefit.

What is the difference between a finance lease and an operating lease?

A finance lease is a long-term arrangement where you effectively take on the risks and rewards of ownership, with an option to purchase at the end. An operating lease is shorter-term, the lessor retains ownership risk, and you return the equipment at the end. Operating leases have lower monthly payments but you never own the asset.

Can I claim tax deductions on leased equipment?

Yes. With an operating lease, the entire payment is typically tax-deductible as a business expense. With a finance lease, you can claim depreciation and interest. With a chattel mortgage, you can claim depreciation, interest, and potentially the instant asset write-off for eligible assets under $20,000.

What interest rates apply to equipment finance in Australia?

Equipment finance rates typically range from 5–12% p.a. for established businesses with good credit. The rate depends on the equipment type, its resale value, your business profile, and the finance structure. New vehicles and mainstream equipment generally attract lower rates than specialised assets.

What is a chattel mortgage and how does it differ from a lease?

A chattel mortgage is a loan secured against the equipment you are purchasing. You own the asset from day one but the lender holds a mortgage over it until repaid. Unlike a lease, you can claim GST credits upfront, claim depreciation, and you own the asset outright once the loan is paid off.
