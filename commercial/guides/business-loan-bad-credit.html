How to Get a Business Loan with Bad Credit in Australia

Published 23 March 2026 11 min read

Having bad credit does not mean your business cannot access finance. While it certainly narrows the field and increases the cost, there are a growing number of Australian lenders who specifically work with borrowers who have impaired credit histories. The key is knowing where to look, what to expect, and how to present your application in the strongest possible light.

According to Equifax data, approximately 1 in 5 Australian adults have some form of negative listing on their credit file. For business owners, this can stem from personal financial difficulties during tough trading periods, disputes with suppliers, or simply from the financial strain that comes with running a small business through challenging economic conditions.

This guide explains what counts as bad credit, which lenders will still consider you, what it will cost, and practical steps you can take to improve your chances of approval.

What Counts as "Bad Credit" for Business Lending?

In Australia, your creditworthiness is assessed through credit reporting agencies: primarily Equifax (formerly Veda), Illion (formerly Dun & Bradstreet), and Experian. Each maintains a credit file that records your borrowing history. The main negative marks that lenders look for include:

  • Defaults: A payment more than 60 days overdue and $170+ is listed as a default. These remain on your file for 5 years (7 years for a clearout). Paid defaults look better than unpaid ones, but both are significant negative marks.
  • Court judgments: If a creditor has obtained a court judgment against you for an unpaid debt. These stay on file for 5 years.
  • Bankruptcy: Listed for 5 years from the date of bankruptcy (or 7 years from the date you are discharged, whichever is later). This is the most severe negative listing.
  • Part IX debt agreements: A formal alternative to bankruptcy. Listed for 5 years.
  • Multiple credit enquiries: While not a "black mark" per se, having many credit applications in a short period signals financial stress and reduces your score.
  • Repayment history: Under comprehensive credit reporting, lenders can see your repayment history for the past 2 years. Late payments (even if they did not become defaults) are now visible.

Which Lenders Accept Bad Credit in Australia?

The Australian lending market has a spectrum of credit appetite. Here is a general guide to which lender types will consider impaired credit:

Major Banks (CBA, NAB, ANZ, Westpac)

Will not approve applicants with defaults, judgments, or credit scores below approximately 600. Their automated systems typically decline applications with any negative listings. Do not waste your time or credit enquiries applying here if your credit is impaired.

Second-Tier and Non-Bank Lenders

Some non-bank lenders will consider minor credit impairment, for example, a single small paid default older than 12 months. They will typically want to see strong business cash flow and may require additional security. Examples include Liberty, Pepper Money, and La Trobe Financial.

Fintech and Online Lenders

Several fintech lenders use alternative assessment methods that weigh business cash flow more heavily than personal credit scores. Some will approve applicants with defaults, provided the business bank statements are clean and show consistent revenue. These include Prospa, OnDeck, Capify, and GetCapital.

Merchant Cash Advance Providers

Merchant cash advance providers are among the most lenient on credit. Because repayments are taken as a percentage of daily card sales, the risk model is based more on your revenue consistency than your credit history. Providers like Capify, MaxFunding, and Lend focus primarily on trading history and turnover.

Invoice Finance Providers

Invoice finance assesses the creditworthiness of your customers rather than your own credit profile. If you have strong commercial debtors (companies that reliably pay their invoices), some invoice finance providers will overlook your personal credit issues entirely. This can be an excellent alternative path to working capital.

Seven Steps to Improve Your Approval Chances

Even with bad credit, there are concrete actions you can take to maximise your chances of getting approved and to minimise the cost:

1. Clean Up Your Bank Statements

Lenders will scrutinise your last 3–6 months of business bank statements. What they want to see: consistent revenue deposits, no dishonoured payments or overdrawn periods, no gambling transactions, and a healthy average balance. If your statements are messy, consider spending 2–3 months cleaning them up before applying.

2. Pay Off Small Defaults If Possible

If you have defaults under $1,000 that remain unpaid, paying them off before applying can make a meaningful difference. A paid default is viewed more favourably than an unpaid one. Request a "paid" notation from the creditor once settled.

3. Gather Strong Supporting Documents

Prepare more documentation than you think you will need: business bank statements (6 months), BAS statements (4 quarters), profit and loss statement, a brief explanation of the circumstances that led to the credit issues, and evidence that those circumstances have been resolved.

4. Offer Security If You Have It

Offering security, whether property, equipment, or a larger personal guarantee, reduces the lender's risk and can significantly improve your chances and rate. Even offering a vehicle or equipment as additional collateral can tip the balance.

5. Start Small and Build a Track Record

If you need $200,000 but your credit is poor, consider starting with a $50,000 loan. Repay it reliably, and you will have a much stronger application for a larger amount six months later. Many specialist lenders actively increase limits for good repayment behaviour.

6. Consider Alternative Products

A traditional term loan might not be available, but a merchant cash advance, invoice finance facility, or revenue-based loan might be. These products assess risk differently and may be more accessible. The right product depends on your business model and cash flow patterns.

7. Use a Comparison Service (Do Not Shotgun Applications)

Every formal loan application creates a credit enquiry on your file. Multiple enquiries in a short period make your credit worse and signal desperation to lenders. Alternative Options When Traditional Loans Are Not Available

If a standard business loan is not accessible due to your credit profile, consider these alternatives:

Merchant cash advance: Ideal for retail, hospitality, and any business with consistent card terminal revenue. Repayments are taken as a small percentage of daily sales, so they flex with your cash flow. Credit requirements are minimal. Providers focus on revenue consistency.

Invoice finance: If your business invoices other businesses (B2B), your customers' creditworthiness matters more than yours. You can access 80–90% of invoice values within days, regardless of your personal credit score.

Revenue-based financing: Some newer fintech lenders offer loans based purely on your business revenue, using bank statement analysis and open banking data rather than traditional credit checks. If your business is generating consistent revenue, these can be an option.

Secured personal loan for business purposes: If you have equity in a property or vehicle, a secured personal loan may be accessible even with impaired credit. Be aware of the risks of using personal assets as security for business purposes.

Rebuilding Your Credit: A Long-Term Strategy

While addressing your immediate funding needs is important, rebuilding your credit should be a parallel priority. Here is a practical roadmap:

  1. Check your credit file for errors. Request free copies from Equifax, Illion, and Experian. Dispute any inaccuracies. Errors are more common than you might think.
  2. Pay all current obligations on time. Under comprehensive credit reporting, your positive repayment history is now recorded and visible to lenders. Every on-time payment improves your profile.
  3. Clear outstanding defaults. Even though a paid default remains on your file, lenders view it significantly more favourably than an unpaid one.
  4. Reduce credit enquiries. Limit new credit applications for 6–12 months. Use comparison services rather than applying directly to multiple lenders.
  5. Build positive history. Consider a small credit facility (even a business credit card) and use it responsibly. Consistent, on-time repayments build positive data on your file.
  6. Wait for negative listings to expire. Most negative marks fall off after 5 years. If your defaults are 3–4 years old, it may be worth waiting before applying for larger facilities.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get a business loan with a bad credit score in Australia?

Yes. While major banks typically require clean credit, several non-bank and fintech lenders specifically cater to borrowers with impaired credit. Options include unsecured business loans, merchant cash advances, and invoice finance. Rates will be higher (typically 18–35% p.a.) but approval is possible.

What credit score do I need for a business loan?

Major banks typically require a personal credit score of 600+ (Equifax scale). Non-bank lenders may approve scores of 400–500. Some fintech lenders and merchant cash advance providers focus more on business revenue than credit scores, meaning even borrowers with scores below 400 may qualify.

Will defaults on my credit file stop me getting a business loan?

Not necessarily. Several specialist lenders will consider applications with defaults. The key factors are: how recent the defaults are, whether they are paid or unpaid, the total value, and the strength of your current business cash flow.

How much more will a business loan cost with bad credit?

Expect to pay significantly more. Where a good-credit borrower might pay 8–15% p.a., a bad-credit borrower will typically pay 18–35% p.a. Establishment fees tend to be higher too (2–4% vs 0–2%). This premium reduces over time as you rebuild your credit through reliable repayment.

How can I improve my chances of approval with bad credit?

Focus on demonstrating strong business cash flow with clean bank statements (3–6 months with no dishonours). Pay off small outstanding defaults if possible. Offer security if available. Start with a smaller loan to build a track record. Use a comparison service like Trade Funding to match with the right lenders without damaging your credit score further.
