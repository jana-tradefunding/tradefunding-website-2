BUSINESS LOANS

How to Compare Business Loans in Australia

Published 23 March 2026 12 min read By Trade Funding

Every year, more than 300,000 Australian SMEs apply for business finance. Yet fewer than one in three borrowers compare more than two lenders before signing a loan agreement. The result? Thousands of business owners end up paying tens of thousands of dollars more than they need to over the life of their loan.

Whether you need $20,000 for working capital or $2 million for a commercial acquisition, comparing business loans properly can save you a significant amount of money and help you avoid products that simply are not suited to your situation. This guide walks you through everything you need to know to compare business loans effectively in 2026.

Why Comparing Business Loans Matters More Than Ever

Australia's business lending market has transformed over the past decade. Where once the Big 4 banks were the only real option, there are now over 80 active business lenders competing for your business. This includes second-tier banks like Judo Bank and Bank of Queensland, online lenders such as Prospa, OnDeck, and Lumi, and specialist providers like ScotPac and Moula.

The range of rates available reflects this diversity. A secured business term loan from a major bank might cost 7.5% to 10% p.a. in 2026. The same borrower could be quoted 15% to 25% from an online lender, or anywhere in between from a non-bank. The difference between a $200,000 loan at 9% versus 18% over three years is roughly $30,000 in interest, money that could be reinvested in your business.

But rate is only one piece of the puzzle. Two loans at the same headline rate can differ dramatically in total cost once you factor in establishment fees, ongoing account fees, early repayment charges, and the way interest is calculated.

What to Look For When Comparing Business Loans

1. Interest Rate and Rate Type

The interest rate is naturally the first thing most borrowers look at, but you need to understand exactly what type of rate you are being quoted:

  • Fixed rate: Your rate stays the same for the entire loan term. This provides certainty for budgeting but typically starts slightly higher than a variable rate.
  • Variable rate: Moves with market conditions, typically benchmarked against the RBA cash rate or the lender's own cost of funds. Lower starting point, but your repayments can increase.
  • Factor rate: Common with short-term online lenders. A factor rate of 1.3 on a $100,000 loan means you repay $130,000 total. This can look cheaper than it is: a factor rate of 1.3 on a 12-month loan translates to roughly 50% to 60% APR.
Key Tip: Always Compare on APR

Ask every lender for the Annual Percentage Rate (APR), which includes the interest rate plus all mandatory fees. This is the only true apples-to-apples comparison metric. A loan advertised at 8% with a 3% establishment fee has a higher effective cost than a loan at 10% with no fees, especially for shorter terms.

2. Fees and Charges

Business loans can come with a range of fees that significantly affect the total cost:

  • Establishment fee: Typically 1% to 3% of the loan amount, charged upfront. Some fintech lenders charge up to 4%.
  • Ongoing monthly fee: Some lenders charge $10 to $30 per month as an account-keeping fee.
  • Early repayment fee: Can range from zero (many online lenders) to the full remaining interest (some bank products). Always check this before signing.
  • Late payment fee: Typically $25 to $50 per occurrence, plus potential default interest.
  • Valuation and legal fees: Relevant for secured loans against property. Budget $500 to $3,000 depending on the asset.

3. Loan Amount and Term

Different lenders specialise in different loan sizes. A lender offering $5,000 to $50,000 is designed for a very different borrower than one offering $500,000 to $5 million. Make sure the lender you are comparing actually offers the amount and term you need:

Lender Type Typical Amount Typical Term Rate Range
Big 4 Banks $50k – $5M+ 1 – 7 years 7% – 12% p.a.
Second-Tier Banks $50k – $3M 1 – 5 years 8% – 15% p.a.
Online / Fintech $5k – $500k 3 months – 3 years 12% – 35% p.a.
Specialist Non-Bank $100k – $5M 1 – 5 years 9% – 18% p.a.

4. Security Requirements

Security is one of the biggest variables in business lending:

  • Unsecured: No asset required. Typically available for loans up to $250,000 to $500,000. Higher rates reflect the lender's increased risk.
  • Secured by property: Residential or commercial property as collateral. Offers the lowest rates but puts your property at risk.
  • PPSR / General Security Agreement (GSA): A charge over your business assets. Common with mid-range lenders.
  • Personal guarantee (PG): You personally guarantee the loan. Almost all business lenders require this for SMEs.

5. Speed of Approval and Funding

If you need funds urgently, whether to take advantage of a supplier discount, cover a tax bill, or bridge a cash flow gap, speed matters. Online lenders like Prospa and OnDeck can approve and fund within 24 to 48 hours. Banks typically take 5 to 20 business days, though some have streamlined processes for smaller amounts.

Types of Business Loans Available in Australia

Before you can compare effectively, it helps to understand the main types of business finance and when each is most appropriate:

Business term loans are the most common form of business finance. You borrow a fixed amount and repay it over a set period with regular repayments. Best for one-off purchases, business expansion, or debt consolidation.

Business lines of credit give you access to a revolving credit facility that you can draw on as needed. You only pay interest on the amount you have drawn, not the total facility. Ideal for managing cash flow fluctuations.

Invoice finance and debtor finance let you access cash tied up in unpaid invoices. Rather than waiting 30 to 90 days for customers to pay, you can draw up to 85% of the invoice value within 24 hours.

Equipment finance is specifically designed for purchasing machinery, vehicles, or technology. The equipment itself serves as security, which generally means lower rates than an unsecured loan.

Merchant cash advances are repaid as a percentage of your daily card sales. There is no fixed repayment schedule: you pay more when business is good and less when it is quiet. Common in retail and hospitality.

Common Mistakes When Comparing Business Loans

Mistake 1: Only Comparing Interest Rates

As discussed, the headline rate is only part of the picture. A loan at 10% p.a. with a 3% establishment fee, $30/month account fee, and a 2% early repayment penalty could easily cost more over its lifetime than a loan at 13% p.a. with no fees at all. Always calculate the total cost of the loan, not just the rate.

Mistake 2: Applying to Multiple Lenders Directly

Each formal loan application typically triggers a credit enquiry on your credit file. Multiple enquiries in a short period can lower your credit score and signal to lenders that you are desperate for finance. Using a comparison service avoids this because only one credit check occurs, when you formally apply with your chosen lender.

Mistake 3: Choosing Based on Speed Alone

Yes, getting funds in 24 hours sounds appealing, but the fastest option is often the most expensive. If you can afford to wait a few extra days, you might save thousands. Plan ahead where possible so urgency does not force you into a more expensive product.

Mistake 4: Ignoring the Loan Contract Terms

Always read the loan agreement carefully, paying particular attention to: what triggers a default, whether the lender can change terms unilaterally, what happens if you want to repay early, and whether there are any restrictive covenants on your business.

Mistake 5: Not Considering Your Future Needs

Taking a short-term loan when you actually need a longer facility, or borrowing too little and needing to refinance within months, can result in paying multiple sets of establishment fees. Think about where your business will be in 12 to 24 months, not just today.
