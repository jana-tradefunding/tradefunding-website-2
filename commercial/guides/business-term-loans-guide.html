BUSINESS LOANS

Business Term Loans in Australia: The Complete Guide

Published 5 April 2026 18 min read By Trade Funding

Business term loans are the most widely used form of commercial finance in Australia. Every year, more than 200,000 Australian businesses take out a term loan to fund growth, purchase equipment, hire staff, or simply keep operations running. Yet many borrowers accept the first offer they receive without understanding the full range of options available to them.

Whether you are a sole trader looking for $20,000 to upgrade your tools, or a mid-market manufacturer seeking $2 million for a new production line, this guide covers everything you need to know about business term loans in Australia in 2026. We explain how they work, what they cost, who qualifies, and how to secure the best possible deal for your situation.

What Is a Business Term Loan?

A business term loan is a lump sum of money provided by a lender that is repaid over a fixed period (the "term") through regular repayments of principal and interest. Unlike a line of credit or overdraft, where you draw and repay funds as needed, a term loan gives you the full amount upfront and you pay it back on a set schedule.

The mechanics are straightforward. You apply for a specific amount, the lender assesses your application, and if approved, the funds are deposited into your business bank account. From there, you make regular repayments (weekly, fortnightly, or monthly) until the loan is fully repaid. Each repayment includes a portion of the original amount borrowed (principal) plus interest.

Key Features of a Business Term Loan

  • Fixed loan amount: You receive the full amount upfront, typically ranging from $5,000 to $5 million depending on the lender and your profile.
  • Set repayment term: Terms range from 3 months to 7 years. Short-term loans (under 12 months) are common with online lenders, while bank loans typically run 2 to 5 years.
  • Regular repayments: Most lenders offer weekly, fortnightly, or monthly repayment options. The frequency can affect your total interest cost.
  • Fixed or variable rate: You may have the choice between a rate that stays constant for the life of the loan or one that moves with market conditions.
  • Security options: Loans can be secured against property, equipment, or business assets, or they can be unsecured, though unsecured loans carry higher rates.

Types of Business Term Loans in Australia

Not all term loans are the same. Understanding the different types helps you narrow down which product suits your business.

Secured vs Unsecured Business Term Loans

Secured business term loans require you to provide collateral: an asset that the lender can claim if you default on the loan. The most common forms of security are residential or commercial property, but lenders also accept equipment, vehicles, and general business assets under a General Security Agreement (GSA) registered on the PPSR.

Secured loans offer significant advantages: lower interest rates (from 7.5% p.a. with a major bank), higher borrowing limits (up to $5 million or more), and longer terms. The trade-off is that your asset is at risk if you cannot meet your repayments.

Unsecured business term loans do not require a specific asset as collateral, although most lenders will still require a personal guarantee from the business directors. Unsecured loans are faster to arrange and involve less paperwork, but they come with higher rates (typically 12% to 35% p.a.) and lower borrowing limits, usually capped at $250,000 to $500,000.

Which Should You Choose?

If you have property or significant business assets and can wait a few extra days for approval, a secured loan will almost always save you money. If you need funds within 48 hours, have no property to offer, or are borrowing under $150,000, an unsecured loan from a fintech lender may be the more practical option. Use Trade Funding's free Compare Report to see both secured and unsecured options side by side.

Fixed Rate vs Variable Rate

Fixed rate loans lock in your interest rate for the entire loan term. Your repayments stay the same regardless of what the Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) does with the cash rate. This provides certainty for budgeting and cash flow forecasting. Fixed rates are particularly valuable when interest rates are expected to rise.

Variable rate loans fluctuate with market conditions, typically benchmarked against the RBA cash rate or the lender's own cost of funds. Variable rates usually start lower than fixed rates but carry the risk of increasing over time. They also tend to offer more flexibility, including the ability to make extra repayments without penalty.

Short-Term vs Long-Term Business Loans

Short-term loans (3 to 18 months) are designed for immediate needs: bridging a cash flow gap, covering a tax bill, taking advantage of a supplier discount, or funding a short-term project. They are typically offered by online and fintech lenders, with faster approval but higher annualised rates.

Long-term loans (2 to 7 years) suit larger investments: commercial property, major equipment, business acquisitions, or significant expansion. These are more commonly offered by banks and specialist non-bank lenders, with lower rates but more stringent eligibility criteria.

Understanding the True Cost: APR vs Headline Rate

Never compare business loans on the headline interest rate alone. Two loans can advertise the same rate but cost vastly different amounts once you factor in fees. The Annual Percentage Rate (APR) captures the total cost of borrowing (interest plus all mandatory fees) expressed as an annual rate. Always ask lenders for the APR, and use it as your primary comparison metric.

Here is a practical example. A $100,000 loan over 2 years:

  • Lender A: 10% interest rate, 2.5% establishment fee, $30/month account fee. True APR: approximately 13.8%
  • Lender B: 13% interest rate, no establishment fee, no ongoing fees. True APR: 13.0%

Lender B is actually cheaper despite the higher headline rate. This is exactly why comparing properly matters, and why Trade Funding's Compare Report calculates the true cost for every lender in your match.

How Much Can You Borrow?

The amount you can borrow depends on several factors, and the range is wide: from $5,000 with an online lender to $5 million or more with a major bank. Here is what drives your borrowing capacity:

Factors That Determine Your Loan Amount

  • Annual revenue: Most lenders use revenue as a primary sizing metric. A common rule of thumb is that you can borrow up to 10% to 20% of your annual revenue with an unsecured loan. Secured lending can go significantly higher.
  • Security offered: If you are offering property as security, lenders will typically lend up to 70% to 80% of the property's value (Loan-to-Value Ratio or LVR). Equipment security is usually valued at 80% to 100% of the purchase price for new equipment.
  • Time in business: Newer businesses have a lower borrowing ceiling. Most banks require at least 2 years of trading history for loans above $250,000. Fintech lenders may lend from 6 months.
  • Cash flow and profitability: Lenders assess whether your business generates enough surplus cash to comfortably service the loan repayments. A common benchmark is a Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) of at least 1.25x, meaning your net operating income is at least 125% of your annual loan repayments.
  • Existing debt: Any current loans, leases, or credit facilities reduce your available borrowing capacity. Lenders look at your total debt exposure, not just the new loan.
  • Credit score: A strong credit score (Equifax score above 622) opens the door to higher amounts and better rates. Scores below 500 significantly limit your options.

Eligibility Requirements

Every lender has its own criteria, but here are the common requirements across the Australian market:

Minimum Requirements (Most Lenders)

  • Active ABN: You must have a registered and active Australian Business Number.
  • Time in business: Banks typically require 2+ years. Fintech lenders may accept 6 to 12 months. Some specialist lenders work with startups that have strong revenue.
  • Minimum annual revenue: Varies widely. Prospa requires $100,000+ per year. Banks look for $200,000+ for unsecured and higher for secured facilities.
  • Credit history: Banks want a clean credit history with no defaults. Fintech lenders are more flexible; many will consider applicants with paid defaults or lower credit scores.
  • No current bankruptcy: Active Part IX agreements or undischarged bankruptcies are deal-breakers for all mainstream lenders.

How to Apply for a Business Term Loan

The application process varies by lender, but follows a similar pattern across the market:

  1. Determine your needs: Before approaching any lender, clarify how much you need to borrow, what the funds are for, how long you need the loan, and what security (if any) you can offer. This prevents you from wasting time on products that do not fit.
  2. Compare your options: Rather than approaching individual lenders one at a time (which risks multiple credit checks), use Trade Funding's free Compare Report to see all your matches in one place. No credit check is required at this stage.
  3. Review your matches: Your Compare Report will show the lenders you qualify with, their estimated rates, fees, loan amounts, and approval timelines. Evaluate the total cost of each option, not just the headline rate.
  4. Prepare your documentation: Gather the required documents for your chosen lender. Having everything ready at the time of application speeds up approval significantly.
  5. Submit your formal application: Apply with your preferred lender. This is the stage where a credit check occurs. If you have used the Compare Report, you should only need to apply once.
  6. Loan assessment and approval: The lender reviews your application, verifies your documents, and may request additional information. Approval timeframes range from same-day to 30 business days depending on the lender.
  7. Loan contract and funding: Once approved, you receive a loan contract to review and sign. After execution, funds are typically deposited into your business account within 1 to 3 business days.

Fees and Charges to Watch For

Business term loans come with various fees that can significantly impact the total cost of borrowing. Understanding these before you sign is essential:

Common Fee Types

  • Establishment fee (also called origination fee): A one-off fee charged when the loan is funded. Typically 1% to 3% of the loan amount for banks and non-banks, and up to 3% to 4% for fintech lenders. On a $200,000 loan, a 2.5% establishment fee adds $5,000 to your upfront cost.
  • Ongoing monthly or annual fee: Some lenders charge a recurring account-keeping fee, usually $10 to $30 per month. Over a 3-year loan, that adds $360 to $1,080 to the total cost.
  • Early repayment fee (break cost): If you repay the loan ahead of schedule, some lenders charge a penalty. This is more common with bank fixed-rate loans, where the fee can be substantial, sometimes the full remaining interest for the term. Many fintech lenders allow early repayment with no penalty.
  • Late payment fee: Typically $25 to $50 per occurrence, plus potential default interest (an additional 2% to 5% on top of your standard rate).
  • Legal and valuation fees: For property-secured loans, expect to pay $500 to $3,000 for a property valuation and potentially legal fees for mortgage documentation.
  • Risk fee or risk margin: Some lenders add a risk premium on top of their base rate for borrowers with weaker profiles. This is not always disclosed upfront, so ask specifically.

Pros and Cons of Business Term Loans

Advantages

  • Predictable repayments make cash flow planning straightforward
  • Large loan amounts available (up to $5M+ with property security)
  • Longer terms spread the cost and reduce monthly repayment burden
  • Wide range of lenders means competitive pricing
  • Interest payments are typically tax-deductible as a business expense
  • Builds your business credit profile for future borrowing
  • Can be used for virtually any legitimate business purpose

Disadvantages

  • You pay interest on the full amount from day one, even if you do not use it all immediately
  • Early repayment penalties can apply, especially with fixed-rate bank loans
  • Secured loans put your property or assets at risk of repossession
  • Bank approval processes can be slow (10-30 business days)
  • Unsecured rates from fintech lenders can be very expensive (up to 35% p.a.)
  • Personal guarantees are required by almost all lenders for SME borrowers
  • Less flexible than a line of credit for ongoing or unpredictable needs

When a Term Loan Is the Right Choice

  • You need a specific amount for a defined purpose (equipment purchase, renovation, acquisition)
  • You prefer predictable, fixed repayments that you can build into your budget
  • You want to spread a large cost over several years to preserve working capital
  • You can offer security and want to access the lowest possible rates

When to Consider an Alternative

  • If your funding need is ongoing and unpredictable, a line of credit gives you more flexibility
  • If your cash flow problems are caused by slow-paying customers, invoice finance addresses the root cause
  • If you just need a small buffer for day-to-day cash flow, a business overdraft may be simpler and cheaper
  • If you are purchasing a specific vehicle or piece of equipment, a chattel mortgage or finance lease may offer better tax treatment

Tips for Getting the Best Business Term Loan Rate

After comparing thousands of business loan applications, here are the most effective strategies for securing a competitive rate:

  1. Compare before you apply. This is the single most impactful thing you can do. The difference between the cheapest and most expensive loan for the same borrower can be 15% or more in annualised terms. Use Trade Funding's Compare Report to see all your options before making a single application.
  2. Offer security if you can. Property-secured loans are consistently 5% to 15% cheaper than unsecured loans. Even if you do not want to use your home, a commercial property or significant business assets can significantly reduce your rate.
  3. Clean up your bank statements. Lenders scrutinise your business bank statements closely. In the 3 to 6 months before applying, reduce gambling transactions, avoid dishonoured payments, and maintain healthy balances. These factors directly affect your risk score.
  4. Prepare your financials. Up-to-date financial statements demonstrate professionalism and make the lender's job easier. Businesses with current, accountant-prepared financials receive faster approvals and often better rates.
  5. Pay off small defaults. If you have any defaults under $1,000 on your credit file, paying them off before applying can meaningfully improve your credit score. Some lenders will not consider any applicant with active defaults, regardless of the amount.
  6. Start with the right amount. Borrowing more than you need wastes money on unnecessary interest. Borrowing too little means you might need to refinance early, paying a second round of establishment fees. Be precise about your actual funding requirement.
  7. Negotiate. Particularly with banks and larger non-bank lenders, rates are often negotiable. If you have a strong application (good revenue, clean credit, property security), push for a better rate. Having alternative quotes (from your Compare Report) gives you leverage.
  8. Consider timing. Some lenders have quarterly lending targets. Applying towards the end of a financial quarter can sometimes result in more competitive offers as lenders push to meet their numbers.
