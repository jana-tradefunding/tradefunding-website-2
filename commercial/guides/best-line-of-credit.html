Best Business Line of Credit Options for SMEs

Published 23 March 2026 11 min read By Trade Funding

Cash flow is the number one challenge facing Australian small businesses. According to the Australian Small Business and Family Enterprise Ombudsman, more than 60% of SMEs experience cash flow problems at some point, and late payment from customers is the leading cause. A business line of credit is one of the most effective tools for managing these fluctuations, but not all facilities are created equal.

This guide covers everything Australian SME owners need to know about business lines of credit in 2026, including what to look for, who offers the best products, and how to qualify.

What Is a Business Line of Credit?

A business line of credit is a revolving facility that allows you to borrow up to an approved limit, repay the funds, and borrow again without reapplying. Unlike a term loan where you receive a lump sum and repay it over a fixed period, a line of credit gives you ongoing access to working capital.

You only pay interest on the amount you have actually drawn, not the full approved limit. For example, if you have a $200,000 line of credit but only draw $50,000, you pay interest on $50,000. Once you repay it, the full $200,000 is available again.

This makes a line of credit particularly well-suited for:

  • Bridging gaps between paying suppliers and receiving customer payments
  • Covering seasonal fluctuations in revenue
  • Managing unexpected expenses without disrupting operations
  • Taking advantage of early payment discounts from suppliers
  • Funding ongoing stock purchases for e-commerce or retail businesses

A business line of credit sits between a term loan and an overdraft. It offers more flexibility than a term loan, but typically has higher limits and lower rates than a charge card.

Who Offers Business Lines of Credit in Australia?

The market for business lines of credit in Australia has expanded significantly in recent years. Here are the main categories of providers:

Big 4 Banks (CBA, Westpac, NAB, ANZ)

All four major banks offer business lines of credit, typically starting from $20,000 to $50,000 and going up to several million. Rates for secured facilities can start from around 8% to 10% p.a. The catch is that approval criteria are strict: most require 2+ years of trading history, strong financials, and often property security for larger facilities. Approval typically takes 5 to 15 business days.

Second-Tier Banks (Judo Bank, BOQ, Bendigo)

These lenders offer rates of around 9% to 14% p.a. with slightly more flexible criteria than the Big 4. Judo Bank in particular has built a strong reputation for business lending with faster turnaround and dedicated relationship managers. Minimum trading history is usually 12 to 24 months.

Online and Fintech Lenders

Providers like Prospa, Finstro, Shift, and Lumi have transformed the market by offering faster approvals and more accessible criteria. Rates are higher (typically 14% to 25% p.a.) but approval can happen in 24 hours to 3 days. Many accept businesses with as little as 6 months of trading history and do not require property security.

Watch Out for Line Fees

Some lenders charge a monthly "line fee" or "facility fee" calculated as a percentage of your total approved limit, not just what you have drawn. For example, a 0.5% monthly line fee on a $200,000 facility costs $1,000 per month ($12,000 per year) regardless of whether you use the facility or not. Factor this into your total cost comparison.

Eligibility Requirements

While each lender sets its own criteria, here are the common requirements you will encounter when applying for a business line of credit:

  • ABN and GST registration: Almost universally required. Most lenders want to see an active ABN for at least 6 to 12 months.
  • Minimum monthly revenue: Banks typically require $10,000+ per month. Fintech lenders may accept $5,000+ per month.
  • Trading history: 6 months minimum for online lenders, 12 to 24 months for banks.
  • Credit history: Clean personal credit helps significantly. Some fintechs accept minor blemishes but not serious defaults.
  • Documentation: Business bank statements (3 to 6 months), BAS statements, and for larger facilities, financial statements and tax returns.

Pros and Cons of a Business Line of Credit

Advantages
  • Only pay interest on what you use
  • Revolving access, no need to reapply
  • Flexible drawdown and repayment
  • Ideal for managing cash flow cycles
  • Can build credit profile over time
  • Funds available instantly once approved
Disadvantages
  • Higher rates than secured term loans
  • Line fees may apply even when unused
  • Lower limits than term loans (typically up to $500k)
  • Annual reviews may reduce your limit
  • Variable rates mean costs can increase
  • Can encourage over-reliance on credit

How to Choose the Right Line of Credit

Selecting the best line of credit for your business comes down to a few key questions:

  1. How much do you need? If you need less than $50,000, online lenders often provide the fastest, simplest experience. For larger facilities ($100,000+), banks and specialist lenders may offer better value despite longer approval times.
  2. How often will you use it? If you plan to use the facility frequently, a lower interest rate matters more. If it is primarily a safety net, watch out for high line fees on unused balances.
  3. How quickly do you need it? If you need access within days, fintech lenders are your best bet. If you can wait 1 to 2 weeks, you may get a better rate from a bank.
  4. What security can you offer? Offering property or a strong personal guarantee can significantly reduce your rate, sometimes by 5% to 10% per annum.

Tips for Managing Your Line of Credit

Once you have a line of credit in place, managing it well is essential for both your cash flow and your credit profile:

  • Set internal limits: Just because you can draw $200,000 does not mean you should. Establish internal policies for when and how much to draw.
  • Repay quickly: Interest accrues daily on most facilities. Repaying drawn amounts as soon as your cash flow allows minimises your interest cost.
  • Monitor your facility: Keep track of your balance, available limit, and any approaching review dates. Lenders can reduce or cancel facilities at annual review if your financial position has weakened.
  • Do not use it for long-term needs: If you find yourself permanently drawing on the facility, a term loan may be a cheaper option for that portion of your borrowing.
Real-World Example

A Melbourne-based wholesaler with $80,000/month revenue needed a $100,000 facility to manage the 45-day gap between paying Chinese suppliers and receiving payment from Australian retailers. Their bank offered $60,000 at 10.5% p.a. with a 0.4% monthly line fee. Through Trade Funding's Compare Report, they also received offers from two fintech lenders: Prospa at $100,000 / 18% p.a. with no line fee, and Finstro at $100,000 / 22% p.a. with no line fee. They chose Prospa. The slightly higher rate was offset by accessing the full amount needed and avoiding the $2,880/year line fee the bank charged.
