What Is a Business Charge Card, and How Is It Different From a Credit Card?

A business charge card looks like a credit card — same network, same tap-and-go — but it works on a different repayment model. The full statement balance is due at the end of every billing cycle. There's no minimum-repayment option and no revolving balance to carry, so there's no interest charge sitting behind it. What you spend, you clear.

That structure changes what the card is for. A credit card gives a business a financing buffer — useful when cash flow is uneven and a balance genuinely needs to be carried some months. A charge card assumes the business can always pay in full, and prices accordingly: the cost sits in a flat annual or monthly fee rather than an interest rate, and in exchange the limits tend to be higher and more flexible because the issuer isn't underwriting a revolving credit risk in the same way.

Employee Card & Expense Management Features

The real advantage of a charge card over a shared company credit card isn't the repayment structure — it's what it lets you do with a team that spends on the business's behalf.

Individual Employee Limits
Set a different spending cap per cardholder, adjustable without affecting anyone else's card or the master account.
Real-Time Spend Visibility
Transactions post to a live dashboard as they happen, not at month-end reconciliation.
Category-Level Blocking
Restrict a card to fuel, materials, or travel merchants only, and decline anything outside that category automatically.
Receipt Capture Integration
Cardholders photograph receipts against each transaction in-app, syncing directly to accounting software.

Understanding the True Cost: Charge Card Fee vs. Credit Card Interest

For a business that always pays its balance in full, a credit card's headline "0% until paid" framing is misleading — the real comparison is the charge card's flat annual fee against the credit card's fee plus the interest that would apply to any balance not cleared by the due date. Worked example: a business running $50,000/year through the card, occasionally missing the due date by a few days on roughly a third of cycles.

BUSINESS CHARGE CARD
$720 p.a. fee
Annual card fee: $720
Interest charged: $0 (no revolving balance possible)
Total annual cost: $720
BUSINESS CREDIT CARD
19.5% p.a. on carried balance
Annual card fee: $295
Interest on ~$4,200 average carried across late cycles: $820
Total annual cost: $1,115

The charge card's flat fee costs $395 less over the year, and the outcome is predictable regardless of a busy month's cash flow timing.

Real-World Case Study

SYDNEY CONSTRUCTION

A Sydney-based commercial fit-out contractor issued charge cards to six site supervisors for materials and fuel purchases across concurrent jobs, replacing a system of reimbursed personal spending and end-of-month expense claims. Category-level blocking limited each card to hardware suppliers and fuel merchants, and the finance manager could see spend against each job in real time instead of waiting for receipts to be submitted. Within the first billing cycle, the real-time dashboard flagged a card being used outside its approved category, catching a $2,300 discrepancy before it was paid out. Reconciliation time dropped from roughly two days a month to under two hours, and the business no longer carried the float of reimbursing staff out of pocket while waiting on receipts.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens if I can't pay the full balance?

Most issuers charge a late fee and may suspend the card until the balance is cleared. Unlike a credit card, there's generally no option to carry a partial balance forward — the facility is built around full repayment each cycle, so repeated shortfalls can lead to the card being closed.

Can sole traders get a business charge card?

Yes, though the panel is narrower than for companies and trusts. Fintech issuers are typically more flexible on structure than banks, provided the business can show 6–12 months of consistent trading history.

Do charge cards affect my personal credit file?

Most business charge cards sit on the business's credit file, not the director's personal file, though a director's guarantee is still required. A formal application does trigger a credit check as part of underwriting.

How many employee cards can I issue?

This varies by issuer — bank-issued cards typically cap the number of supplementary cards, while fintech and corporate card platforms are usually built to scale to dozens of cardholders with individually set limits.

What spending categories can I block?

Most platforms allow blocking by merchant category code — fuel, hardware, travel, entertainment, and so on — so a card can be restricted to only the categories relevant to that employee's role.

Is a charge card better than a business credit card?

It depends on cash flow. A charge card is typically cheaper and offers higher limits for a business that reliably pays in full each cycle. A business that sometimes needs to carry a balance is better served by a credit card, which is built for that.

What if I need to carry a balance occasionally?

A charge card isn't built for this — a business line of credit or overdraft is a better fit for occasional short-term cash flow gaps, since both let you carry a drawn balance and pay interest only on what's used.
