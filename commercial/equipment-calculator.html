Frequently Asked Questions

With a finance lease, the lender owns the equipment and you make regular payments to use it. At the end of the lease, you typically pay a residual value (usually 10% of the asset cost) to take ownership. With an equipment loan, you own the asset from day one and repay the full principal plus interest over the term. Loans generally have higher monthly payments but no balloon payment at the end.

It depends on your situation. Leasing offers lower monthly payments (due to the residual) and may provide tax benefits since lease payments are typically fully deductible. Buying via a loan means you own the asset outright at the end and can claim depreciation. Leasing suits businesses that upgrade equipment frequently, while buying is better for long-term assets that hold their value.

A residual (or balloon) payment is a lump sum due at the end of a finance lease. It represents the estimated remaining value of the equipment. Typically set at 10 to 30% of the original asset cost, it reduces your regular payments during the lease term. At the end, you can pay the residual to own the asset, refinance it, or return the equipment.
