Calculator FAQs

What is the difference between a finance lease and an equipment loan?

With a finance lease, the lender owns the equipment and you make regular payments to use it. At the end of the lease, you typically pay a residual value (usually 10% of the asset cost) to take ownership. With an equipment loan, you own the asset from day one and repay the full principal plus interest over the term. Loans generally have higher monthly payments but no balloon payment at the end.

Is it better to lease or buy equipment for my business?

It depends on your situation. Leasing offers lower monthly payments, due to the residual, and may provide tax benefits since lease payments are typically fully deductible. Buying via a loan means you own the asset outright and can claim depreciation. Leasing suits businesses that upgrade equipment frequently, while buying suits long-term assets that hold their value.

What is a residual or balloon payment on a finance lease?

A residual, or balloon, payment is a lump sum due at the end of a finance lease. It represents the estimated remaining value of the equipment. This calculator uses a fixed 10% of the original asset cost, though lenders can structure residuals anywhere from 10% to 50% depending on the asset. At the end, you can pay the residual to own the asset, refinance it, or return the equipment.

Can I claim tax deductions on leased or financed equipment?

Generally yes, but the treatment differs. With a finance lease you can typically claim depreciation on the asset and the interest component of the payments. With an equipment loan you can claim depreciation, interest, and potentially the instant asset write-off for eligible assets. Always confirm the current rules with your accountant before deciding.
