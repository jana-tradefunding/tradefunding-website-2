FAQ

Frequently asked questions

A charge card requires you to pay the full balance each billing cycle (typically 30–55 days), while a credit card allows you to carry a balance and pay interest. Charge cards offer interest-free float but no option to revolve debt. This makes them ideal for businesses that want working capital flexibility without the temptation or cost of carrying balances.
Business charge card limits typically range from $10,000 to $500,000 depending on the provider and your business profile. Some providers like American Express offer higher limits for established businesses. Your Compare Report will show what each provider offers based on your revenue and credit profile.
Most providers require an active ABN, at least 6 months of trading history, minimum annual revenue of $100,000, and good personal credit for the director. Some newer fintech providers have more flexible requirements. Your Compare Report will match you with providers you’re likely to qualify for.
Rewards vary by provider and can include points programs (redeemable for travel, gift cards, or merchandise), cashback on spending, and travel perks like lounge access and travel insurance. American Express is known for premium rewards, while newer fintechs like Capital on Tap focus on cashback simplicity.
Yes. Modern business charge cards often include expense management features like employee sub-cards with individual spending limits, real-time transaction notifications, receipt capture via mobile app, accounting software integration, and detailed spending reports. Providers like Weel and Airwallex specialise in this area.
